From the source · Tavares 2015, pp. 103–104 · translated from Spanish

The buildings of this period kept the same spatial and ornamental features as those of the previous decade. One example is the stately urban residence at Puebla 112, by Eudoro Urdaneta and Julio Corredor Latorre, from 1921.

Its most striking features are the large cartouches with garlands and pendants of flowers and acanthus leaves, set as keystones in the window lintels. The ironwork of the ground-floor windows and of the main entrance door stands out for its magnificent design and good state of preservation.

The brackets have square heads and fluted shafts, with small bell-shaped flowers in half relief and a base of guttae. Beneath the cornice there are thick dentils and a row of egg mouldings.

Plant motifs intertwine on the jambs of the entrance door, and at the top there is a kind of pre-Hispanic panel with six guttae between its sections. Above a small cornice carried on two brackets with cylindrical heads sits a large oculus, almost entirely surrounded by leaves, with a short bracket as its keystone.
